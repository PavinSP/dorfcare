Maria, and the numbers behind her

Speaker 1

Maria is 82 and lives alone in Werneck, twelve kilometres from Schweinfurt. She doesn't drive. When her doctor retires and nobody takes over, a simple check-up means a taxi, or waiting until it becomes an emergency.

Maria is fictional. The numbers around her aren't. In five municipalities around Schweinfurt, 9,925 people are over 65. In the GP planning area Schweinfurt-Süd, five GP seats are already empty, and 15 of the 36 GPs are 60 or older. The gap is coming.

And more than half of people over 78 lack basic digital skills. So the answer can't be one more app.

1:45 – 2:30

Who pays, and where the money comes from

Speaker 3

Care has a price, and ours is small: three ninety-nine a month per household, often paid by the family. SOS and 112 are never behind a paywall.

Municipalities fund the local network. We're testing about 400 euros per village per month. That stays below the 100,000-euro limit, so a mayor can award it without a tender.

The care itself stays billed to insurance, as today. For people with a care grade, the care insurance pays 131 euros a month for everyday help, and from 2027 home visits by practice assistants are paid outside the GP budget. We plan those visits.

To build it, we'll apply for EXIST or FLÜGGE stipends, the Start-Zuschuss, and an Innovationsfonds project with THWS.

2 minutes of jury questions

Q&A cheat sheet

Is a doctor in the village hall even legal?

Yes, as a branch practice (Filiale, §24 Abs. 3 Ärzte-ZV). The GP practice or an MVZ applies and KVB approves before the first patient; each doctor can hold at most two branches. Until approval the day runs as video plus a nurse at home, so patients don't wait for paperwork.

Isn't an app that handles health a medical device?

We stay outside the medical device rules on purpose: no triage, no diagnosis, no health scores. If someone mentions chest pain, the app only asks "Do you want 112?" and the person decides.

Schweinfurt isn't officially underserved. Why here?

True, coverage is 95.85 to 107.55%. But in Schweinfurt-Süd five GP seats are open and 15 of 36 GPs are 60 or older. We build the network before the gap, not after.

What if there aren't enough volunteers?

That's the main reason these schemes fail, so we plan for it: small tasks, a cap per helper, a paid coordinator, a legal allowance under §45b, and a search radius that widens from 5 to 10 to 15 km. Medical work goes to professionals anyway.

Why would a GP join?

Only medical work reaches the doctor. Rides and errands go to volunteers, vitals and wound care to nurses. Home visits by practice assistants are paid outside the budget from 2027, and Bavaria pays a €10k mobility bonus for mobile practice staff.

Who are your competitors?

DorfFunk (now StadtLand.Funk) is the village app in Bavaria, without health features, so it's a partner. Hausnotruf providers are partners too: their human operators handle alarms and 112. Nobody coordinates volunteers and professional care together.

What about data protection?

A volunteer sees the exact address only after accepting, and only until the task is done. Medical details go only to doctors and nurses, never to family or volunteers. For a pilot: a data-protection impact assessment and an EU host with a C5 attestation.

Emergencies: does the app call 112?

Never automatically; that's forbidden in Germany (§4 NotrufV). The person or a human operator at the alarm-button partner starts the call.

How big is the market?

244 municipalities in Lower Franconia and 1,482 in Bavaria have fewer than 5,000 residents. At our test licence price that's a ceiling of about €1.2M a year in Lower Franconia and €7.1M in Bavaria from licences alone. On top come households: if one in five people over 75 in our five pilot municipalities subscribes, that's about 930 households, or roughly €45k a year at €3.99 (an illustration, not a forecast).

Why should an 82-year-old pay anything?

€3.99 a month is less than one coffee, and usually the family pays it. SOS and 112 are always free, and the coordinator helps anyone who calls the hotline. Nursing and doctor visits stay billed to insurance as today, so nobody pays twice for care.

Is Clara replacing human contact?

No. Clara does the 9 a.m. check-in call and keeps people company, always says she's an AI, and gives no medical advice. When someone is lonely, she offers a real volunteer visit.
