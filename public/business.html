DorfConnect business case

Who pays, who we sell to, how this becomes a company rather than an app, and how well the prototype answers the challenge. Researched 27 Nov 2026; every number links to its source, and our own price guesses are marked hypothesis.

The thesis in one paragraph

DorfConnect is not a consumer app. It is the care-coordination service a village is missing. Municipalities, GP practices and the care insurance already pay for pieces of rural care: rides, home visits, everyday help, emergency alarms. Nobody coordinates those pieces. We sell the coordination layer (software, a local coordinator and a vetted volunteer network) to the municipality. We earn more from the statutory money that flows through it, and residents use it for free.

What one village cluster could look like hypothesis

A Verwaltungsgemeinschaft of 3 villages: licence 3 × €400 × 12 = €14,400/yr, well under the €100k direct-award limit. A pilot with 10 villages in one Landkreis stays near €48k/yr. If every small municipality in Lower Franconia took the licence, that is 244 × €4,800 ≈ €1.2M/yr; across Bavaria, 1,482 × €4,800 ≈ €7.1M/yr, before care-insurance, family and practice revenue. These are ceilings to sanity-check the model, not forecasts.

6 · Why a startup

Why this is a company, not an app

Operations, not only code

Each village needs a coordinator, vetted volunteers and partner contracts. That local work is the product and is hard to copy.

Village network effects

More volunteers mean faster help; lasting volunteer–resident relationships and trust scores build up per village.

Several payers

Municipal licence plus statutory care money plus families plus practices. Not dependent on one grant; Medibus and the kiosks show why that matters.

Compliance as a moat

AVSG recognition, C5-attested hosting, DPIA, an intended purpose outside the medical-device rules, insurance. Slow for a newcomer to copy.

Integration hub

Connect what exists (StadtLand.Funk, Hausnotruf, Rufbus/Callheinz, practice software, e-prescriptions) instead of rebuilding it.

Outcome data

Check-ins kept, rides delivered, time to appointment, loneliness scores. That evidence opens Innovationsfonds money and insurer contracts.

Bavaria, then other states, using the pilot evidence for selective insurer contracts.

How we sell and market

  • Sales to municipalities is personal: mayors talk to mayors. Win one respected village, film it, and present at district mayors' meetings and Gemeindetag events.
  • Fit their existing plans: Bavarian districts write senior-policy plans (seniorenpolitisches Gesamtkonzept). Position DorfConnect as a ready-made measure for them (legal basis to verify).
  • Recruit volunteers where they already are: clubs, parishes, fire brigades, schools, THWS service-learning.
  • Families: local press (Main-Post), and messages to adult children ("Know Mum is fine every morning").
8 · Must-dos and risks

Before a paid pilot

  1. Intended purpose: coordination and wellbeing, not medical. No triage, no scoring of anyone's health. Red-flag words only ask "Do you want 112?" (now built). Get a written opinion on whether the medical-device rules apply (MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1).
  2. Data protection: explicit consent for health data, separate consent for location, a DPIA, and an EU host with a C5 attestation (§393 SGB V).
  3. Emergency calls: a person always starts the 112 call, either the resident or a Hausnotruf centre; automatic calls are not allowed (§4 NotrufV).
  4. Volunteers: operator liability insurance, cover for drivers, ID check, certificate of good conduct, training, code of conduct (Ehrenamtsversicherung applies only after other cover).
  5. AI companion: Clara always says she is an AI (EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency, in force since Aug 2026) and never uses manipulative engagement.

Biggest risks

  • Too few volunteers. Small tasks, caps per helper, a paid coordinator, and §45b allowances.
  • Slow, small municipal budgets. Stay under the direct-award limit; co-fund setup with KoFöR.
  • Grant dependency. Medibus and the kiosks ended with their funding; stack several payers from day one.
  • Feature creep into medicine. Keep doctors and nurses in charge of anything clinical; we provide the logistics.
  • Incumbent adds health. Smartes Land could add similar features; partner early.
